Paper inspection records work. They have worked for decades and, kept properly, they do their job: recording who inspected what and when.

The problem with paper is not reliability. It is that **it does not answer questions**.

## What paper cannot do

With a folder of records you can answer "when was this rope last inspected?" if you find its sheet. What you cannot answer without spending an afternoon on it:

- Which gear is overdue for inspection **right now**?
- Which items did that person inspect before stepping down?
- How many harnesses have we retired this year, and for what reasons?
- Which gear was on loan the day of that fall?

These are management questions, not filing questions. And they are exactly the ones that come up when something goes wrong.

## What you gain

**Queries, not searches.** The real change is not storing the same thing somewhere else: it is being able to ask.

**Reminders.** An inspection due on 14 October announces itself. On paper, it expires in silence.

**Continuity.** The history stops living in a folder somebody keeps at home.

**A link to usage.** This is what paper almost never achieves: cross-referencing inspection history with loan history. Knowing that the item inspected in March has been out twelve times since changes the decision about whether to wait for the annual inspection.

## What is worth keeping from paper

Digitising badly is worse than not digitising. Three things from paper deserve to survive:

**The signature of whoever inspects.** A **person** must be recorded, not "the club". If the system allows inspections to be recorded without identifying who did them, it has lost the part that gave the record its value.

**The free-text notes field.** Closed forms with tick boxes are convenient right up until someone needs to write "the sheath has a stiff section 12 metres from the green end". That detail is what matters six months later.

**Immutability.** A signed record is not rewritten. If your system lets you edit a past inspection without leaving a trace, you have swapped a record for a draft. Corrections are appended, not substituted.

## How to migrate without losing what you have

**Do not type in ten years of paper.** It is enormous work and, for gear already near retirement, pointless.

What works:

1. **Scan the history** and store it as a file attached to each item. It becomes accessible without having been transcribed.
2. **Start the digital record at the next inspection.** From then on, everything new is digital.
3. **Transcribe only two fields** from paper: date of last inspection and date first put into service. With those the system can already calculate due dates, which is what is most needed.
4. **Keep the paper for a while.** Do not bin it on day one. A year of overlap is reasonable.

## The underlying mistake

Many clubs digitise by copying the paper record verbatim into a form. The result is slower paper: the same fields, the same manual searching, and now you also need your phone to hand.

Digitising pays off when the record stops being a document and becomes a consequence. The inspection is recorded **while inspecting**, the loan **while lending**, and the due date is calculated by the system. If there is still a separate moment of "filling in the record", you have not digitised anything.
